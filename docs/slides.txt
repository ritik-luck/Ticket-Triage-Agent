"The stack is layered — React on top, FastAPI for API, then the Agent orchestrates RAG, tools, guardrails, memory, and analytics. The frontend has zero AI logic — cleanly separated."

"Frontend has TicketForm, ResultPanel, and admin components. The Agent internally orchestrates cache, RAG, tools, guardrails, SLA, and memory."

"For knowledge grounding, I built a real RAG pipeline — CSV to loader, chunking, embeddings, ChromaDB, retriever, context injection, and Groq LLM generates the answer."


Slide 10 — Why RAG (10 sec)

"Why RAG? Because LLMs alone hallucinate. Grounding responses in real knowledge with a confidence threshold is what makes the system trustworthy."


Slide 11 — Workflow Diagram (15 sec)

"Here's the full workflow — from ticket submission through cache check, RAG retrieval, LLM answer, classify, priority, route, guardrails, SLA prediction, save, and return."

"Each stage has a clear purpose — Why, When, Where, and How. Nothing is placed randomly."


Slide 13 — Sequence Diagram (10 sec)

"At runtime, the request flows through 15+ steps across the agent, RAG, tools, guardrails, SLA, and memory — everything traceable."


Slide 14 — Data Flow (10 sec)

"Data moves cleanly from inputs to decisions to output — with the guardrail + SLA layers now sitting right before the response."


Slide 15 — Class View (10 sec)

"I designed structured classes — models, agent, tools, RAG, memory, analytics — and added a new SLAManager class for prediction and escalation."


Slide 16 — Guardrails + RBAC Overview (10 sec)

"Guardrails override priority/team for critical cases. RBAC keeps sensitive data admin-only."


Slide 17 — Deterministic Guardrails (15 sec)

"My first version used keyword matching — simple and explainable, but it missed variations. If a user wrote 'app not reachable' instead of 'outage,' the guardrail failed."


Slide 18 — Model-Based Guardrails ⭐ (20 sec)

"So I upgraded to model-based guardrails. Now an LLM classifies the ticket into categories like production outage, security incident, data loss, or business-critical failure — returning structured JSON with type, severity, and confidence."


Slide 19 — Hybrid Guardrail Flow (15 sec)

"The final design is hybrid — rule-based first for speed, model-based after for context. Only if confidence is above 0.70, the guardrail triggers."


Slide 20 — Guardrail Example Scenarios (10 sec)

"Here are real ticket examples — outage, security, data loss, and a normal password reset. Only the critical ones trigger guardrails."


Slide 21 — Why Guardrails Matter (10 sec)

"Guardrails are the AI safety net — they prevent silent misrouting of critical tickets."


Slide 22 — SLA Prediction: What & Why ⭐ (20 sec)

"The next feature I added is SLA prediction. SLA means the promised time to respond or resolve a ticket. Not every ticket is equal — some cannot wait. The system now predicts SLA risk so admins can act early."


Slide 23 — SLA: How It Works (15 sec)

"It takes priority, category, and guardrail result as input, and outputs SLA hours, risk level, escalation required, escalation team, and a reason — all as structured JSON."


Slide 24 — Escalation Logic (10 sec)

"Escalation follows clear tiers — Critical goes to Incident Response, sensitive categories go to specialized teams, low priority stays in normal support."


Slide 25 — SLA + Escalation Examples (15 sec)

"For example — a production outage gets a 2-hour SLA and Incident Response Team. A password reset gets 24 hours and normal support. The system knows the difference."


Slide 26 — Confidence, Cache & Fallback (10 sec)

"For reliability, I added cache to avoid recomputation, a confidence score to signal trust, and fallback logic when internal KB is weak."


Slide 27 — Memory & Audit Trail (10 sec)

"Every ticket logs the full trail — RAG score, guardrail source, SLA decision, escalation team, and reason. This is what makes AI decisions reviewable."


Slide 28 — Admin Analytics Dashboard (10 sec)

"The admin dashboard shows ticket volume, RAG vs fallback ratio, guardrail counts, and SLA risk distribution."


Slide 29 — RBAC Deep Dive (10 sec)
